# Project Management Fundamentals — Study Notes

A study summary covering core project management concepts: definitions, roles, planning tools, risk strategies, and lifecycle phases.

---

## 1. Definition of a Project

**A project is a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result.**

Unlike ongoing daily operations, a project has:
- A clear **beginning and end** (temporary)
- A goal of producing something **new and unique**

---

## 2. Key Skills of a Project Manager

A project manager does **not** need deep technical expertise in every project area. What matters more:
- **Leadership** — guiding the team
- **Negotiation** — resolving competing interests
- **Communication** — keeping everyone aligned

> Technical mastery of all disciplines involved is **not** a core PM competency.

---

## 3. SMART Goals — "M" = Measurable

Goals should include a way to **measure progress numerically** so success can be objectively verified. Without measurability, it's hard to know if a goal has actually been achieved.

(Full SMART = Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)

---

## 4. Project Charter

The **Project Charter** is the document that:
- Formally **authorizes** the project
- Grants the **project manager authority** to use resources and begin work

Think of it as an official "birth certificate" or launch permit for the project.

---

## 5. Project Resources

Project resources = **people, materials, and finances** — all tangible inputs needed to execute the work. It's not just about money; labor and equipment count too.

---

## 6. First Step in Building a WBS (Work Breakdown Structure)

Step one: **establish the major segments of work** (break the project into a few large chunks) *before* drilling down into smaller details.

---

## 7. Risk Response Strategies

Common strategies: **Avoid, Mitigate, Transfer, Accept**

| Strategy | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Avoid | Eliminate the risk entirely |
| Mitigate | Reduce likelihood/impact |
| Transfer | Shift risk to a third party (e.g., insurance) |
| **Accept** | Acknowledge the risk but take **no proactive action** |

**Accept** is used when a risk has low impact or when prevention would cost more than the risk itself.

---

## 8. Gantt Chart

**Purpose:** to visually show the **project schedule and task dependencies** — using bar graphs to display when tasks start/end and how tasks depend on each other.

---

## 9. Communication Management Plan

Defines how project information flows among stakeholders. Typically includes:
- **Roles and responsibilities**
- **Communication methods** (who tells whom, how, and when)

---

## 10. Project Baseline

The **approved version** of scope, schedule, and cost — used as the reference point ("answer key") to measure actual project performance against the plan.

---

## 11. Project Closure — Readiness

A project is ready for **formal closure** when:
- **Deliverables are accepted** by the client
- **Administrative tasks** (paperwork, documentation) are complete

---

## 12. Estimating Activity Duration

Formula: **Duration = Work Effort ÷ Number of Resources (people)**

More people assigned → shorter duration to complete the same total workload.

---

## 13. Project Lifecycle — Charter Approved

When a **Project Charter** has just been approved and the PM is preparing the project management plan → the **Initiation** phase has just been completed.

(Initiation → Planning → Execution → Monitoring/Controlling → Closure)

---

## 14. Statement of Work (SOW)

Document the **client** typically provides during initiation, describing:
- Work to be performed
- Objectives
- Major deliverables
- Technical requirements
- Expected completion dates

The sponsor uses this SOW to draft and approve the **Project Charter**.

---

## 15. Project Lifecycle Phases — What Doesn't Belong

Standard lifecycle phases: requirements → design → development → testing → deployment → closure.

**Marketing** is **not** typically a lifecycle phase — it's an external, post-completion business/promotional activity, not part of the internal process of building the deliverable.
